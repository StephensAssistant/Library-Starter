# Writing Fragment Capture

Preserve rough observations and arguments as reusable source fragments without prematurely forcing an article structure.

## When to use and intent routing contract

Activate for capture raw writing fragments, organise article idea fragments or equivalent intent. Exclude publish raw notes, treat a fragment as a verified claim. Own this technique only; orchestration, approvals, release and communications remain with their current owners.

## Method

1. Capture the user’s wording, example, source and intended insight with minimal editorial change. Distinguish quotation, paraphrase, observation and unverified claim.
2. Give each fragment a small descriptive label and topic/argument cue; preserve context needed to interpret it. Group related fragments without erasing contradictions or inventing connective claims.
3. Keep raw fragments separate from developed drafts and verify factual claims before reuse. Maintain the existing canonical capture location; avoid an additional parallel note store.
4. When shaping begins, identify which fragments support a thesis and which remain unused. Preserve provenance and the user’s voice; route draft creation through writing style and document owners.

## Anti-patterns

- Turning every note into polished prose, fabricating citations, or making a raw claim sound verified.
- Auto-publishing or deleting unused fragments.

## Benchmark cases

- Positive: Capture a concrete debugging observation and its source before deciding its place in an article.
- Negative/boundary: An unsupported statistic remains labelled unverified rather than promoted into the draft.

## Stop

Stop when fragments are retrievable with context and provenance. Article structure and delivery remain separate work.

Upstream marks this method beta/in progress. This is a bounded Library adaptation, not evidence of production tooling reliability. Verify it against the actual task before relying on it.
